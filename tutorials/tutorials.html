Vis Journal Text Visualization Tutorial

(Journal Data Dashboard in the manuscript)

In this tutorial, we will demonstrate the application of the Vis Journal Text Visualization for analysis of students’ reflection journals collected as a formative assessment. Students taking a data visualization course were asked to respond to prompts in reflection journals on a weekly basis over a 10‑week quarter. These journals were collected digitally with students’ consent and uploaded to the website demonstrated below for analysis and visualization. The course is an introductory level course at an American, primarily undergraduate university. After the tutorial, there are some evaluation questions about the usefulness and ease of use.

Summary page

  • In the Summary page, you can inspect students’ responses to prompts (top‑left) at every time point (e.g., each week in this data). The prompts asked at each time point are shown on the top‑left. Frequency of words in students’ responses are summarized in a word cloud (bottom‑left). Individual student’s responses are shown in the table (right), along with a set of key phrases generated from each response.
  • You can select a certain week or prompts that you want to inspect. You may scroll to read the full prompts.
  • The word cloud at the bottom‑left categorizes the words commonly used by students by part of speech: nouns (blue), verbs (orange), and adjectives (green). You can click one or multiple words in the word cloud to highlight the word(s) used in students’ responses (right). Note that words with the same stem in the same part of speech (e.g., work and working as verbs) will be highlighted simultaneously.
  • You can click a word again to deselect the word.

WordStream page

  • In contrast to the Summary page in which students’ responses each week are visualized separately, the WordStream page displays the frequency of words in students’ responses over several time units (e.g., weeks) in one chart, i.e., the WordStream (top‑left). The prompts asked at each time point are shown on the top‑right. Sentences in individual students’ responses are shown in the table (bottom).
  • You can select a certain week to recall the prompts (top‑right). Note that this action does not alter the WordStream.
  • The WordStream reveals the common nouns (blue), verbs (orange), and adjectives (green) that students used in their responses over time. The bigger a word, the more times students used the word in their responses in a certain week. You can click a word in the WordStream to produce a line (or “stream”) of varying widths which represents the frequency change of that word in students’ responses over time. When you do this, the word(s) used in students’ responses, and words that share the same stem in the same part of speech (e.g., work and working as verbs) will be highlighted simultaneously (bottom).
  • To deselect a word in the WordStream, click in the white space above or below the highlighted stream (top‑right).
  • You can inspect multiple words in the WordStream simultaneously by clicking one word per part of speech. For example, you can click work as the verb, data as the noun, and visual as the adjective. When you do so, student responses (bottom) will be filtered to show only the sentences containing all selected words.
  • To inspect the use of words that are not in the WordStream or two words in the same part of speech in the WordStream, you can use the search box (bottom‑right) that serves as a secondary filter for the individual student responses (bottom).

Evaluation

After following the tutorial and using the Vis Journal Text Visualization , evaluate the ease of use and usefulness of the journal visualization by addressing the following questions with an explanation that is at least 2 sentences.

Ease of Use

  • As an instructor, how well does the app allow you to explore the details of the students’ responses? Explain.
  • As an instructor, how well does the app allow you to identify broad trends among the students’ responses? Explain.
  • As an instructor, how well would the app help you make instructional decisions related to student progress? Explain.
  • As an instructor, how easy is it to use the app? Explain.

Usefulness

  • As an instructor, to what extent does the app make it easier to assess student progress? Explain.
  • As an instructor, to what extent does the app give you insight into the development of student ideas over time? Explain.
  • As an instructor, to what extent does the app allow you to see the diversity in your students’ responses and experiences? Explain.
